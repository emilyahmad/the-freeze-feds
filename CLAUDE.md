# The Freeze Feds

Web game: a crowd of Mii-style people mingles around a cartoon SF intersection (California & Powell). Hover anyone and their head swells up and turns to look at you (like Find Mii); click someone to call them up to the camera for an ID check (they hold up their LinkedIn-style profile on a phone), then hit **Freeze** under the phone, which opens the webcam: mime a snowball throw (make a fist, bring it toward the camera and open the hand to show the palm as it gets close) that freezes them in ice (a wrong person isn't frozen: they throw a snowball right back at you, with the −10 s penalty); click anywhere else to let them go. 60 s round, +10 s for a right catch, −10 s for a wrong one, ends at 0 s or when all 9 bosses are frozen.


- Stack: Vite + Three.js (vanilla JS). `npm install`, `npm run dev` → http://localhost:5173. `npm run build` → `dist/` (relative paths via `base: './'`, ready for GitHub Pages / itch.io).
- `public/street.webp`: the background, hand-drawn SF art (billboard, cable car, Golden Gate, painted ladies) painted over the original street photo at exactly 2× (3344×1882; converted from the teammate's 7 MB `street.PNG`), so the crosswalks, camera match and `WALK_AREA` (still in the original 1672×941 pixels, `IMG_W`/`IMG_H`) line up unchanged. `#stage` fills the whole window: the photo (`#photo`, holding the `<img>` and anything pinned to it like `#walk-sign`) is scaled to cover it and centred, cropping the overflow, and `main.js` `layout()` gives the camera the same crop with `setViewOffset` so the 3D crowd stays lined up (`imgToGround` in scene.js ignores that crop). Look-around: the photo is drawn `LOOK_OVERSCAN` (5%) bigger than cover and `updateLook()` eases the crop (photo + camera view offset together) toward the cursor at `LOOK_EASE`, so the view drifts like a head turn without leaving its spot; it's held still on the title screen and starts when a round does (`lookOn`). On very narrow (portrait) windows the sides are cropped, so the person at the ID-check spot can be partly off-screen. `src/signal.js` (`createSignal()`: `walk()` / `stop({ blink })`) drives the crossing signal post `#walk-sign`: the teammate's hand-drawn art (`design-references/crosswalk-art/signal-pole.png`, `signal-hand.png`, `signal-walk.png`, all on one 4096×3072 canvas) cropped to the same box into `public/signal/pole|hand|walk.webp` and stacked as `<img>`s (pole, then the orange hand `.ws-hand` or white walking man `.ws-man`, each with a CSS drop-shadow glow; `#ws-face` is an empty div over the pole's black face, used by the intro zoom), pinned to `#photo` where the original photo's pole stood (style.css: base centred at original-photo x 1396 on the road at y 402, just past the houses, pole 329 px tall). If the art is redrawn, re-crop all three with the same box so the lights stay on the face. The hand as drawn is much smaller than the walking man, so `hand.webp` is scaled up 2× and the walking man shrunk to 75%, so both are 200 canvas px tall, each centred on the man's original box (canvas px 2057..2197 × 1117..1383) before cropping. The steady hand shows before the first round, the WALK man from the intro on and during a round (with the tick, `sfx.walkLoop`), and when a round ends the hand blinks for a moment, then holds steady until Play again.
- `src/scene.js`: camera matched to the photo by eye (`CAM_HEIGHT`, `CAM_PITCH_DEG`, `CAM_FOV`) and the walkable area: a polygon in **image pixels** (`WALK_AREA`) covering the far sidewalks, far crosswalk and foreground intersection down to `WALK_BOTTOM`, projected onto the ground plane. `buildWalkArea()` gives containment/clamping and random destinations (`randomPoint()` samples over the image, biased toward the bottom by `NEAR_BIAS`, so people come toward the camera; `randomNear()` picks a spot a few metres away in any direction). Lower `WALK_BOTTOM`/higher `NEAR_BIAS` = closer to the camera, but anyone within ~4 m walls off the street. `?debug` skips the start screen, draws the grid and the area's outline, shows the image-pixel coordinates under the cursor and exposes `window.__game`.
- `src/mii.js`: loads the Mii GLBs from `public/models` (bodies, heads, hair, hats; see its README) before the crowd spawns, and `buildMii()` assembles one: a `SkeletonUtils` clone of the skinned body (shirt split into torso/sleeve/hand and pants into legs/shoes so each gets its own colour), a head shape with ears, a canvas-drawn Wii-style face (almond eyes with coloured irises, tapered brows, shaded nose, two-tone lips, optional moustache/beard/stubble/wrinkles/blush/lashes; drawn at `FACE_SCALE` around `EYE_Y`) projected on with `DecalGeometry`, and a hairstyle or hat. The ripped meshes are welded and re-smoothed at load (Miis are smooth, not faceted). `setBone`/`setChildBone` rotate bones in the character frame (x right, y up, z forward) on top of the T-pose. Shading is soft glossy Phong (`SHINE` in characters.js), like the Wii Miis; outlines are inverted hulls pushed out along normals in the shader (they follow the skinning) and are hidden except as the hover highlight and the frozen-in-ice tint (`userData.setOutline`). The models are git-ignored, so the game needs them in `public/models` to run. The base Mii follows `design-references/fixed_mii.glb`: the head sits `HEAD_LIFT` above the neck bone so a short neck shows, and its fitted default hair is `public/models/character-hair/hair-default.glb` (extracted by `model-sources/extract_hair.mjs`). `buildMii({ headWidth })` > 1 widens the head for a broad face. The ripped hairs sit too tight on some heads (scalp pokes through): `HAIR_HEAD_LIFT` raises hair on the taller heads (03/07/08) and `HAIR_FIT` scales individual hairstyles up about `HAIR_PIVOT`; add a hair there if a bald spot shows (hair-32/33 are balding on purpose).
- Photo faces: `src/mii.js` wraps real photos from `design-references/tech-faces/` onto the front of the Mii head (a `DecalGeometry` per head shape, `photoDecal`) instead of the drawn face. `PHOTO_FACES` maps each boss id to its file with hand-measured eye centres and chin (image pixels), so every photo is scaled/rotated to put its eyes on the Mii eye line; `softEdges` and `PHOTO_OVAL` feather the photo into the head, and the head/ears/hands are tinted to a skin tone sampled from the photo. Photos with glasses drop the 3D glasses; one boss's VR headset is pushed up onto his forehead. Anyone without a photo keeps the drawn Mii face; crowd photos in `design-references/remaining-faces/` (AI-generated `rando*.jpeg`, eyes/chin in `CROWD_FACES` in mii.js) go to the background Miis without repeats per round (`resetFaceDeck`), and `CROWD_LOOKS` in characters.js dresses each one to match its photo (gender, hair model/colour, clothes, a smaller `scale` for the kids, hover-card `info`); extra photos in `tech-faces/crowd/` (optional `f-`/`m-` prefix) also join the pool. Assistants keep their uniform look.
- `src/characters.js`: `makePerson(look)` builds a Mii from a look (colours, `head`, `hairModel`, `face`, `gender`) and hangs the accessories on it: glasses, headsets and earbuds on the head frame, collars, ties and scarves on the chest frame, props in the hand frame. `CEO_TYPES` lists the 9 bosses: a fixed `look` (hair style, outfit, prop via `carry`), the hover-card `info` line and a `behavior`; `CEO_MII` picks their head, hairstyle and face. Boss looks follow the characteristics table the user gave (hair, glasses, face shape, facial hair, outfit) on top of the `fixed_mii` base; the general Mii style follows `../be-elsa/design-references/pictures-of-miis.jpg`. Face extras include `goatee`, `beard`, `moustache`, `stubble`, `wrinkles`, `bushy` brows. `pose()` drives the bones (legs, upper arms and forearms, head) plus a social overlay from crowd.js (`fx`: talking gestures, waving, nodding, laughing, head turn); `encase()` freezes. crowd.js relies on the `userData` contract (`rig`, `mats`, `outlineMat`, `hitbox`).
- `src/crowd.js`: agents and behaviours. At the start of a round (`reset()`) everyone walks in from just past the left or right edge of the photo (`area.offscreen()` in scene.js, at a random depth, `ENTER_MARGIN` m out, staggered by up to `ENTER_SPREAD` s, `ENTER_SPEEDUP`× brisk) while `entering` (`enterStep`; not clamped to the area, not chatted to or pitched at), and starts their usual routine (`startBehavior`) once well inside; assistants come in with their boss. Behaviours: `wander` (normals: roam to random destinations, `NEAR_CHANCE` of them close by, and change their mind mid-walk at `RETARGET_RATE`), `pace` (phone pacer), `lead` + `follow` (CEO with two assistants), `pitch` (stops to pitch at passers-by), `speed` (fast walker), `slow` (ambles), `loiter` (long still pauses). Normals sometimes wear a single CEO item (phone, vest) as decoys. Collisions: everyone is a solid circle (`RADIUS`; frozen bosses are bigger immovable ice, `ICE_RADIUS`). `avoid()` steers walkers around people ahead, `collide()` pushes overlaps apart after each step (standing people are heavier than walkers), a hard push starts a lean-away wobble (`bump()`), and anyone blocked for `STUCK_TIME` picks a new target. Walking speeds are `WALK_SPEED` and friends (≈0.75 m/s), with the step rate matched to speed (`STEP_RATE`). Mingling (like Miis in the Mii Channel/Plaza): wanderers form chat circles of up to `CHAT_MAX` (`seekChat`/`layoutGroup`/`chat`/`updateGroups`), take speaking turns with hand gestures while listeners look at the speaker, nod and sometimes laugh, then wave goodbye; passers-by sometimes wave (`greet`); people idle-glance around. Bosses don't mingle, so their tells stay readable. Hover (after `design-references/hover-on-mii.png`): `setHover(a)` marks the person under the cursor; `headZoomStep` eases their `headFrame` up to `HOVER_HEAD`× from the neck (hitbox taller by `HOVER_HIT`) and `updateFx` turns their head toward the camera (up to `HOVER_NECK`) while they keep walking; the person being checked doesn't swell. Carrying (after `design-references/dragging-by-head.jpg`, like grabbing a Mii in the Mii Plaza): `grab(a)` / `carryTo(a, groundPoint)` / `drop(a)`; while `carried` they stay at the depth they were picked up at (freeze.js casts the cursor ray onto the upright plane at their z, so the drag only moves them sideways and up/down, kept on the street along that line), are lifted `CARRY_LIFT`..`CARRY_LIFT_MAX` m (following the cursor up), hang from their eyes (`hangH`, person rotation order `ZXY` so the swing is about world x/z) and swing like a pendulum against the drag (`SWING_K`/`SWING_W`/`SWING_D`), kick their legs and wave, face the camera, a cloned ground shadow (`carryShadow`) stays under them, and they're out of collisions/chats/greets; let go and they fall (`fallStep`, `DROP_G`), wobble and resume their routine from there. ID check: `inspect(a, onPresent)` walks someone to `INSPECT_SPOT` in front of the camera, `release(a)` sends them back (hurrying at `RELEASE_SPEEDUP`× for `RELEASE_HURRY` s; clicking off someone releases them instantly and checks whoever was clicked; released people stay marked with `markReleased()`: a green wash `RELEASED_TINT` and mint outline `RELEASED_OUTLINE`, after `design-references/released-mii-outline.png`, which `setHighlight` restores after hovering), `freezeAndReturn(a)` freezes them and slides the ice block back home. Flinch: as the webcam throw gets past `BRACE_FROM` of the meter (freeze.js, via ThrowCam's `onCharge`), `brace(a, k)` makes the checked person start to cower, and `flinch(a, time)` holds a full cower while the snowball flies (people within `FLINCH_NEAR` m flinch too, weaker and a beat late); `fx.flinch` in `pose()` raises both fists in front of the face, ducks and turns the head away and bends the knees, crowd.js adds a lean back and a tremble, and `freeze()` keeps the flinch so they're iced mid-cower. When a round ends `exit(keep)` sends everyone not frozen (and not in `keep`) briskly (`EXIT_SPEED`) off the nearer side of the photo (`leaving`/`exitStep`) and removes them there, so the street empties under the end screen. Finale: if time runs out with bosses still loose, they're kept and `lineUp(bosses, onLined)` hurries them (`LINE_SPEED`) into a row across the far crosswalk (image px `LINE_X`/`LINE_Y`, `LINE_GAP` m apart, `LINE_GIVEUP` s cap; `finale`/`finaleStep`) facing the camera; `throwSnowball(a, onRelease)` (also used by an innocent at the ID-check spot to throw back) then shows a snowball in their left hand and plays an overhand throw (`fx.throw` in `pose()`, release at `THROW_RELEASE` in characters.js; crowd.js adds the lean and shoulder twist, `THROW_WIND`/`THROW_TWIST`) followed by a smug laugh. Freezing (`freezeAndSendOff`) leaves the boss in ice for the stamp, then the block slowly slides off down the street (`SENDOFF_TIME`, `SENDOFF_DIST`), spins gently and fades out (`FADE_FROM`, on per-person cloned materials so shared ones don't fade), and is then retired: out of the scene, hitboxes and collisions, but kept in `crowd.retired` so the end screen still lists them as frozen.

## Roadmap (original plan)

The first playable build (steps 1–6 below with placeholder people) is done. The placeholder people have since been replaced with Mii models (`src/mii.js`). Next up: animated background.

Here's a one-day plan. It's web-based, uses free tools only, and uses Three.js for the game itself.

### Stack

- **Game:** Vite + Three.js (vanilla JS), with `@pixiv/three-vrm` to load avatars. No engine install or export step, and the result is a link you can send people.
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages or itch.io (both free).
- **Avatars:** VRoid Studio (free) makes the characters and exports `.vrm` files.
- **Walk animation:** Mixamo (free Adobe login). Download one walk cycle and retarget it to every VRM. The `three-vrm` repo has a Mixamo example you can copy.
- **Street background:** a free SF photo from Unsplash or Pexels. Check the license.

### The 3D model problem

VRoid is anime-styled, so it won't look like a Mii out of the box. You can get close with a big head, simple eyes and a chibi-proportioned body. It's also slow: about 15–30 minutes per character, so 20 unique ones would eat most of your day.

To avoid that:

1. Hand-make **3–4 base bodies** and **~6 CEO characters** in VRoid. Each CEO should have a signature costume or prop.
2. Get the rest of the crowd by cloning the bases with `SkeletonUtils.clone`. Randomize shirt, skin and hair colors by tinting materials in code, and toggle hats, glasses and bags as child meshes.
3. For a more Mii-like look, use a toon outline or a flat-shaded material in Three.js. This is cheaper than remodeling.

### Core build, in order

1. **Scene (1–1.5h):** Put the street photo in a plain `<img>` behind a transparent WebGL canvas. Match the camera angle by eye, then draw a ground plane and waypoint paths along the sidewalks and crosswalks. When you swap in the animated GIF later, you only change the `<img>` source.
2. **Crowd (1.5h):** Spawn ~20 clones. Each picks a random waypoint, walks to it, pauses, then picks another. Scale each one by its depth on the screen so far ones look smaller.
3. **Hover inspect (1.5h):** Raycast against each character's hitbox. On hover, show a card with a close-up of that character. For the close-up, render them with a second camera into a small render target. Add an info line like "Patagonia vest, phone in hand."
4. **Capture (1h):** On click, check `isCEO`. If it's a CEO, freeze the animation and tint them (ice or handcuffs). If not, play a "wrong" flash.
5. **Round logic (1h):** The timer starts at 120s. A correct capture adds 10s and a wrong one removes 10s. The round ends at 0s or when all CEOs are caught. Add a start screen and a score screen.
6. **Polish (1–2h):** Add sound effects (free from freesound.org or Pixabay), a hover highlight outline and a restart button. Then deploy.

### Making CEOs identifiable both visually and by behavior

- **Visual tells:** The CEOs get distinctive costumes and props.
- **Behavior tells:** Give each type its own movement script:
  - Pacing while staring at a phone.
  - Walking with a flanking entourage.
  - Stopping to "pitch" at passersby.
  - Walking faster than everyone else.
- Normal Miis share the same random-walk behavior.

### Parodying real CEOs

Stick to costume and prop hints, like a signature outfit or accessory. Skip real names and recognizable face likenesses, and add a "parody" label. Satire is generally on solid ground, but that's not legal advice, so be more careful if you plan to monetize. Also, "Mii" is Nintendo's trademark. For a public release, give the game and characters a different name.

### Order of work

Do the walking crowd with placeholder capsule people first, and only then swap in VRoid models. If you run short on time, you still have a playable game.
